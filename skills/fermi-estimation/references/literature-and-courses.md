# Literature — Fermi Estimation

Reference list only — not loaded by default. Sources synthesized into the SKILL.md's core principles.

## Origin

| Work | Author | Core contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Trinity test yield estimate (1945), from paper-scrap blast-wave drift distance | Enrico Fermi | The archetypal real-world Fermi estimate — a quick, decomposed order-of-magnitude calculation that landed close to the true value |
| "How many piano tuners are there in Chicago?" (classroom problem) | Enrico Fermi | The canonical teaching example that gives the technique its name |

## Modern Treatments and Handbooks

| Work | Author | Core contribution |
|---|---|---|
| *Guesstimation: Solving the World's Problems on the Back of a Cocktail Napkin*, 2008 | Lawrence Weinstein & John A. Adam | Modern popular handbook of worked Fermi problems and decomposition technique |
| *Street-Fighting Mathematics: The Art of Educated Guessing and Opportunistic Problem Solving*, 2010 (MIT Press, open access) | Sanjoy Mahajan | Formalizes decomposition and dimensional-analysis estimating as a general problem-solving discipline |
| *How to Measure Anything*, 2007 | Douglas Hubbard | The "decomposition defeats 'unmeasurable'" principle applied to business quantities — direct link to `forecasting-and-measurement` |
