# Literature and Free Training — Forecasting, Actuarial Science, and Measurement

Reference list only — not loaded by default. Availability of specific free/legal sources may change; verify before relying on a link. Recent (post-2000ish) trade books are under copyright and generally not legally free — use a library app (Libby/OverDrive) or purchase; items marked **(free)** are genuinely free at time of writing (public domain, publisher-released, or open-access).

## Foundational Forecasting Psychology

| Work | Author | Core contribution |
|---|---|---|
| *Superforecasting* | Philip Tetlock & Dan Gardner | Traits of top forecasters: probabilistic thinking, decomposition, outside view, incremental updating, active open-mindedness |
| *Expert Political Judgment* | Philip Tetlock | The original research base behind *Superforecasting* — hedgehogs vs. foxes; experts often underperform simple statistical models |
| *The Signal and the Noise* | Nate Silver | Applied forecasting across domains (weather, elections, markets, earthquakes, epidemics) — shows accuracy is domain-dependent, not a universal skill |
| *The Black Swan* | Nassim Nicholas Taleb | Fat-tailed ("Extremistan") domains where rare unmodeled events dominate outcomes; critiques overconfidence in statistical forecasting |
| *Antifragile* | Nassim Nicholas Taleb | Designing for robustness/gain-from-disorder instead of prediction accuracy; barbell strategy, optionality |
| *Fooled by Randomness* | Nassim Nicholas Taleb | Earlier, more accessible treatment of randomness misattributed to skill |
| *Range* | David Epstein | Generalists often out-forecast specialists in fast-changing, low-data domains |
| *Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment* | Daniel Kahneman, Sibony, Sunstein | Unwanted variability between judgments of the same case (as distinct from bias); decision hygiene techniques |
| *Thinking, Fast and Slow* | Daniel Kahneman | System 1/System 2, anchoring, availability, base-rate neglect, planning fallacy |

## Measurement and Decision Analysis

| Work | Author | Core contribution |
|---|---|---|
| *How to Measure Anything* | Douglas Hubbard | Anything can be measured by reducing uncertainty; calibrated estimation training; value of information |
| *The Failure of Risk Management* | Douglas Hubbard | Critiques common corporate risk-matrix practices; ties back to actuarial/statistical rigor |
| *The Flaw of Averages* | Sam Savage | Why planning around a single average number systematically misleads under uncertainty |
| *Thinking in Bets* | Annie Duke | Decision quality vs. outcome quality; resulting bias; probabilistic thinking applied to decisions |
| *The Art of Statistics* | David Spiegelhalter | Accessible, rigorous overview of statistical reasoning and its limits |

## Actuarial and Statistical Technique References

| Resource | Author/Body | Core contribution |
|---|---|---|
| *Forecasting: Principles and Practice* (FPP3) | Rob J. Hyndman & George Athanasopoulos | **(free, otexts.com/fpp3)** — the standard open textbook on time-series forecasting: ETS, ARIMA, STL decomposition, cross-validation, forecast combination |
| *Loss Models: From Data to Decisions* | Klugman, Panjer, Willmot | Core actuarial exam text — frequency/severity modeling, credibility theory, loss distributions |
| M-Competition papers (M1–M4) | Spyros Makridakis et al. | **(free, published papers)** Large-scale empirical forecasting competitions; key finding: simple methods are hard to beat, combination methods reliably help |
| SOA / IFoA exam syllabi (e.g., Exam P, FM) | Society of Actuaries / Institute and Faculty of Actuaries | **(free syllabi/study notes)** Structured, free curriculum outline for probability, financial math, and actuarial modeling even without sitting the exam |
| Extreme Value Theory primers (Coles, *An Introduction to Statistical Modeling of Extreme Values*) | Stuart Coles | Standard reference for tail-risk / catastrophe modeling technique |

## Free Hands-On Platforms and Publishers

- **Good Judgment Open** (goodjudgment.com) — make real calibrated forecasts, get scored with a Brier score against actual outcomes; the closest thing to a public credential in this field.
- **Metaculus** — same idea, heavy focus on AI/tech questions; reading top forecasters' public reasoning is itself training.
- **CSET** (Georgetown Center for Security and Emerging Technology) — free reports and methodology on tech trend forecasting, often with underlying data.
- **Epoch AI** — free published research/data on AI compute and capability trend extrapolation; a worked example of real trend-forecasting methodology in one specific domain.
- **WEF Future of Jobs Report** (free, annual) — useful to read several years back-to-back and check past predictions against what actually happened, to calibrate how reliable this genre of forecasting really is.
- **FPP3** (otexts.com/fpp3) — listed above; the only item on this page that doubles as both a free book and a hands-on course (includes R code and exercises).

## Non-Degree Short Courses

- Coursera "Business and Financial Forecasting" / "An Introduction to Forecasting" — shallow but fast primers on ARIMA/exponential smoothing.
- CFI (Corporate Finance Institute) forecasting/financial-modeling certificates — structured syllabus, not prestigious, useful as a checklist.
